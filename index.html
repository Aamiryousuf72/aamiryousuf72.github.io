1 — Biography

About me

I am a final-year PhD candidate in the Department of [Department Name] at [University Name], working under the supervision of [Advisor Name]. My research sits at the intersection of numerical analysis and scientific computing, where I develop discretization schemes and solvers for partial differential equations that describe coupled physical processes — for example, fluid–structure interaction, thermo-mechanical coupling, or reaction–diffusion systems evolving in time.

My work focuses on three connected questions: how to discretize PDEs so that the resulting schemes remain stable and accurate (numerical methods for PDEs); how to couple solvers for different physical fields without sacrificing consistency or efficiency (coupled multiphysics problems); and how to march these systems forward in time reliably, especially when timescales differ across components (computation of time-dependent problems).

Numerical Methods for PDEs

Finite difference, finite element, and finite volume schemes; stability and convergence analysis for elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic problems.

Coupled Multiphysics Problems

Monolithic and partitioned strategies for interacting physical fields — interface conditions, stability of coupling schemes, and iterative solvers.

Time-Dependent Computation

Time-stepping schemes, adaptive time integration, and error control for long-horizon and multi-timescale simulations.
